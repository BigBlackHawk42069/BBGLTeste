API Technical Transparency

A precise breakdown of every network call this script makes, for anyone who wants to verify the plain-language disclosure against the actual behavior. Torn allows roughly 100 API calls/minute and a 50,000-row/day log read cap per category, shared across every script you run. Everything below is measured against those limits.

Key Scope & Log Types

The Create API Key button generates a key pre-scoped to exactly six log categories, plus battlestats and basic faction data. Nothing broader is ever requested.

user: battlestats
faction: rankedwars, basic
logids: Strength (56), Speed (52), Defense (54), Dexterity (50), Item Use (23), Points (6), Faction (80)

Within these categories, the specific log types actually read are:

» Training (4 log types): strength, defense, speed, dexterity.
» Energy items (6 log types): green eggs, Xanax, LSD, energy cans, hotel coupons, points refills.
» Stat enhancers (4 log types): parachutes, skateboards, boxing gloves, dumbbells.
» Happy items (4 log types): candy, erotic DVDs, ecstasy, yellow eggs.
» Overdoses (3 log types): Xanax, LSD, and Ecstasy ODs.
» Ranked Wars: for placing war periods on the calendar.
» Faction Application Accepts: read only once during the backfill process to accurately align your past war history.

Big Black Backfill

Big Black Backfill is a manual, one-time catch-up tool. You trigger it yourself, and once your history is fully reconstructed there's nothing left to run. It exists purely to backfill older history or gaps that regular sync doesn't reach back for on its own.

Torn caps cloud-data log reads at 50,000 rows per day per category, shared across every script you use, not just this one. Big Black Backfill budgets itself well under that ceiling, keeping it well clear of locking you out of other tools.

Staying Within Torn's Limits

Every part of this script is built to stay quiet next to whatever else you run on Torn. Regular sync sits at a fraction of a percent of Torn's allowed rate even under heavy use, and Big Black Backfill paces itself well under the daily cap. The goal is a script that stays out of the way of other tools, and holds up the same way years from now as it does today.
